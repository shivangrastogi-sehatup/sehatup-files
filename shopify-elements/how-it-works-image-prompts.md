## What went wrong on the first attempt (2026-09-17)

Three prompts were tried and all three came back as the same thing: flat
corporate vector clip art, the house style of every AI illustration today.

- **The dial** read as a car dashboard, with three navy bars under it that
  looked like a hamburger menu icon.
- **The person** came back as a cartoon mascot: big hair blob, thumb on chin,
  the stock "thinking" pose used on every SaaS landing page.
- **The body map** was a dark navy silhouette with leader lines to blank boxes,
  which reads as a forensic or anatomy diagram. Grim, and cold.

The fault was in the brief, not the tool: "flat 2D vector illustration, clean
geometric shapes" **is** the description of that look. The medium has to change.

## Direction A - cut paper

Torn and layered paper, photographed flat. Tactile and warm, and the one medium
that cannot be mistaken for vector art. Works at 200px because paper shapes are
big and simple by nature.

Prompts are written as plain prose with no headings, labels or bullet symbols.
Image models follow a described scene better than a spec sheet, and a pasted
spec sheet often comes back as a diagram of itself.

### Step 1 - Know Your Health Score

```
Make a cut paper collage illustration, photographed flat from directly above, for step 1 of 4 in a health brand's how it works strip. The step is called Know Your Health Score and it covers a short assessment of lifestyle, symptoms and goals. Show three small paper cards lying in a loose fan, as if someone has just answered three questions and set them down. On the card nearest the viewer, place one mark torn from crimson paper: a short upward stroke, like a tick made by hand. On each of the two cards behind it, place one small torn navy mark. That is four marks in total across the three cards. No faces, no figures, no devices.

The medium is the whole point of the image. Use real paper with hand torn edges that show white fibre where they tear, and clean scissor cut edges elsewhere. Every piece is a separate layer casting a soft, very short natural shadow onto the piece below, the kind you get from paper lying one millimetre above paper under diffuse daylight. Keep a slight paper grain visible, let the cards sit very slightly askew rather than perfectly aligned, and use matte stock with no gloss.

Use paper stock colours only: warm off white faf7f6, blush fdf2f4, pale blush f7d9df, deep navy 14183c and crimson ee204a. Crimson appears on one small mark only and covers no more than a tenth of the image.

The background is plain flat white and fills the entire square canvas, corner to corner. Do not place the collage on a coloured circle, disc, rounded card, panel or any other background shape, and do not cut a notch or bite out of anything. The page already supplies a pink circle behind this artwork, so any circle drawn into the image will collide with it.

Centre the cards in the canvas with roughly equal space on all four sides, keeping a generous empty margin at the top left corner and along the right edge, because a number badge sits over the top left and an arrow points out of the right.

This image will be seen at 200 by 200 pixels, so keep it to six to eight paper pieces in total, each one big and legible, with nothing thinner than four percent of the canvas width. Avoid rows of repeated small dots or stacked bars of different lengths: at small size they read as an interface icon rather than paper.

Do not produce a flat vector look, cartoon characters or mascots. No gradients, no glow, no long drop shadows, no photorealistic 3D rendering, no text or numbers anywhere, no fake interface, no gauge, dial or speedometer arc, no stethoscope and no medical cross.
```

## Direction B - one continuous ink line

A single unbroken pen line, the way a doctor sketches on a prescription pad.
Confident, quiet, premium. Scales down perfectly because it is one stroke.

```
Make a single-line ink drawing: one continuous unbroken stroke, drawn without
lifting the pen.

SUBJECT - step 1 of 4 in a health brand's "how it works" strip, titled
"Know Your Health Score": a short assessment of lifestyle, symptoms and goals.
The line traces a person's head and shoulders in profile and, without lifting,
continues out of the back of the head into a rising curve that ends in a dot.
One gesture: a person, and the measure that comes out of them.

MEDIUM - this is the whole point of the image:
A real pen line on paper. Even weight throughout, about 6% of the circle width,
with the small wobbles and pressure changes of a hand-drawn stroke, not a
mathematically perfect bezier. Rounded ends. One line only: it may cross itself
but it must never break.

COLOURS:
  The line: deep navy #14183c
  The dot at the end of the line: crimson #ee204a, filled, about twice the line
  width. It is the only filled shape and the only crimson in the image.
  Background: plain white, nothing else. No fill inside the face or shapes.

COMPOSITION:
Centred inside a circle covering 76% of a square canvas, with generous empty
space around the drawing - at least a fifth of the circle on every side. The
top-left eighth of the circle stays empty. Nothing important in the right 8%
of the canvas.

FACE: the profile is suggested by the line alone. One small notch for the nose,
one for the lips. No eye, no eyebrow, no hair strands, no ear detail, no smile.

SIZE RULE: this will be seen at 200 x 200 pixels. If a curve would be smaller
than a grain of rice on a phone, straighten it out.

BANS: no flat vector clip art, no cartoon character, no mascot, no filled
silhouette, no shading, no hatching, no gradients, no glow, no shadow, no text
or numbers, no device, no stethoscope, no medical cross, no second line.
```

## Direction C - risograph print

Two-colour risograph, the way Indian small-press health pamphlets are printed.
Grainy ink, slight misregistration, colours that overlap and darken. Warm,
human, and visibly printed rather than generated.

```
Make a two-colour risograph print illustration.

SUBJECT - step 1 of 4 in a health brand's "how it works" strip, titled
"Know Your Health Score": a short assessment of lifestyle, symptoms and goals.
Show a pair of hands resting on a simple printed form, one hand holding a pencil
mid-tick. Hands and form only, seen from above at a slight angle. No face, no
body, no phone, no screen.

MEDIUM - this is the whole point of the image:
Risograph printing on uncoated paper. Visible ink grain and speckle, flat solid
areas that look slightly starved of ink in places, and one colour printed a
hair out of register with the other so edges show a 1-2px offset. Where the two
inks overlap they darken into a third tone. No smooth gradients anywhere: any
tonal change is a coarse halftone dot pattern.

COLOURS - exactly two inks plus the paper:
  Ink 1: deep navy #14183c - the drawing, the hands, the form lines
  Ink 2: crimson #ee204a - the pencil tick and nothing else
  Paper: warm off-white #faf7f6
Crimson covers no more than a tenth of the image.

COMPOSITION:
Centred inside a circle covering 76% of a square canvas, white outside it. The
top-left eighth of the circle stays empty. Nothing important in the right 8%
of the canvas.

HANDS: simple and blocky, no individual knuckles, no nails, no rings, no watch,
no sleeve pattern. Four strokes for the form's lines, no text on it.

SIZE RULE: this will be seen at 200 x 200 pixels. Six to eight elements total.
Nothing thinner than 4% of the circle width.

BANS: no flat vector clip art, no cartoon character, no mascot, no smooth
gradients, no glow, no drop shadow, no 3D rendering, no text or numbers, no
fake interface, no stethoscope, no medical cross.
```

## Judging

Look at all three at 200px, on white, before deciding. Ask:

- Would a designer believe this was commissioned rather than generated?
- Does it still read at arm's length on a phone?
- Can steps 2, 3 and 4 be drawn in the same medium? All three extend cleanly:
  cut paper makes a kit and a calendar easily, the single line carries a
  stethoscope and a repeat loop, riso handles a doctor and a delivery box.
